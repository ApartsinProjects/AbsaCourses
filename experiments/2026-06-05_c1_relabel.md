# 2026-06-05 — C1 relabel-and-retrain (NEGATIVE / not for paper)

**Question:** does cleaning the synthetic LABELS (per the at-scale audit) improve the
SYNTHETIC-ONLY model, measured by transfer to real Herath? Cleaning = clean-by-removal:
detection target kept only for audit-SUPPORTED aspects; sentiment target kept only where
audit SUPPORTED & polarity-MATCHED, else masked.

**Setup:** bert-base-uncased two-step, transfer to real Herath 9-aspect. Synthetic corpus
restricted to rows with >=1 overlap aspect (6,867/10k -> ~5,495 train), matching
evaluate_synthetic_to_real_transfer.restrict_to_overlap. RunPod RTX 4090 (SECURE), seed 42.

**Result (seed 42, matched harness baseline vs treatment):**
| condition | det micro-F1 | sentiment MSE (detected) | train det labels |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (original labels) | 0.318 | 0.349 | 7,395 |
| treatment (cleaned labels) | 0.264 | 0.463 | 6,305 |
| paired delta (treat - base) | -0.055 | +0.113 | (worse on both) |

**Conclusion (NEGATIVE):** label-level removal HURTS synthetic-only transfer. Stripping
individual unfaithful labels converts uncertain aspects into hard negatives and removes ~half
the polarity training signal, weakening the detector. This is the OPPOSITE of §5.9's
ROW-level faithfulness filtering (keep faithful rows whole), which improves transfer sentiment
MSE (+0.054, CI excludes zero). Granularity matters: row-filtering helps, label-removal hurts.

**Caveat:** the C1 harness is a reduced reimplementation that does NOT reproduce the paper's
0.4593 synthetic-only transfer (baseline 0.318), so the absolute is not comparable to the
manuscript. The paired delta is valid only within this harness.

**Disposition:** DIAGNOSTIC ONLY — NOT folded into the paper (wins-only rule). The paper's
"filtering improves the dataset" claim rests on §5.9 row-filtering. Stopped here: the
label-removal hypothesis is falsified; a "correct-the-polarity" variant would need a new
audit pass (auditor's read polarity) = C2/regeneration territory, not pursued.
